## Still open

- **Limit-aware coaching.** Four of five writers invented the limit cues (head position under a delicate neck, groin-safe ranges, the injured foot's place at each station). An optional per-area coaching line would close it.
- **Circuits should group floor and bench stations** when getting up and down is costly (c41: sixteen transitions).
- **Sprint rest** in the intervals dose is under the new four-times rule (c34).
- **Last-set segments** hold only the work, which rushes the count (c20, c38, c40); one-sided slow moves need more seconds per rep.
- **Machine variety** in conditioning: hard bike moves now fill most blocks in a gym with other machines (c32).
- **Kneeling positions** (`cat-cow`, `thread-the-needle`) carry no foot load though the top of the foot bears weight; with the toes untucked the load is light, so they stay as they are.
- Smaller: where to sit for the seated speed bag after a machine block; easier and harder versions for arm circles and shoulder rolls; round four's unexplained extra seconds in interval circuits.

### What the scores say

Interval blocks for conditioning now prefer hard movements, a real efficacy gain (c32, c34), but block selection should rotate machines when the kit has them. ## Judge 2: c36, c38, c39, c40, c41

### The five lowest cases

By the sum of the four scores, with safety breaking ties:

1. **c41 (14).** The skeleton chose sound movements under a hard limit: no standing work, one-sided bridge and side plank, everything else seated or lying. But it alternated floor, bench and floor stations, giving sixteen transitions across four rounds that each risk a push off the injured foot. It also printed the supine twist (dropping the knees toward that side rolls the injured foot onto its outer edge, the position the ankle's limit guards against) and kneeling moves with no ankle or foot load, and its Coaching setups assume both feet working ("feet flat on the floor" for the bench press, "stacked or staggered" for the side plank). The writer caught all of it. Fault: **skeleton** (station order, catalog loads, setups that don't account for limits), with the script doing the rescue.
2. **c40 (15).** The rule that picks paired sets when straight sets would drop a pattern brought in an anti-extension slot (dead bug) for a glutes target, and the pair slots' seconds can't hold eleven reps of two movements plus 90 s of rest, especially the last set (eleven split squats a leg in two 25 s holds). Fault: **skeleton** (method rule applied to a targeted goal; set seconds against rep doses).
3. **c36 (16).** It is still a timed strength circuit for an advanced conditioning request. The seated speed bag is the only hard station, so the delicate low back leaves the hotel room with no other fast move that spares the back. The sided station and the round-four extras still have to be improvised. Fault: **skeleton and catalog**. The script is good.
4. **c38 (17).** It is fine overall. The last pair-set and the last bench-set segments hold only the work, so the counts are rushed, and six row sets is modest for a back target. Fault: **skeleton** (last-set timing), minor.
5. **c39 (17).** It is fine. Box breathing comes three times because four breathing slots plus the cool-down share three breathing drills, and the warm-up is 270 s of marching in a full gym. Fault: **skeleton**, minor.

### What the scores say

**The catalog**
- **Ankle and foot loads for kneeling and floor positions.** `cat-cow` and `thread-the-needle` (kneeling) carry no ankle or foot load, yet kneeling presses the top of the foot into the floor with the ankle pointed. `supine-twist` carries none, yet dropping the knees to one side inverts that foot. Under an off-limits ankle, these are offered without warning (c41).
- **Foot drive in lying presses.** `dumbbell-bench-press` has no foot load, but its setup says "feet flat on the floor", and leg drive goes through them. The `side-plank` setup offers "staggered" feet, which loads the top foot. A limit-aware setup line, or a support load, is missing (c41).
- **Advanced hotel-room conditioning under a delicate low back.** The seated speed bag adds one hard station, but there is still only one (c36).
- **Coaching notes.** They visibly improved rep definitions, feel lines and fault fixes, but they have no variants for limits. Four of the five writers had to invent the limit cues themselves: the neck head position on bent-over rows (c38), groin-safe ranges (c39), low-back cues for the bridge and squat (c36), and the injured foot at every station (c41). An optional per-area line (`limits.<area>`) in the coaching fields would close this. Arm circles and shoulder rolls have no Easier or Harder line (c39).

**The dose rules**
- **The last set of a pair, or of straight sets, gets only its work time.** At three or four seconds a rep this is too short: c38's bench set 3 is 36 s, and c40's last pair set holds eleven split squats a leg in two 25 s holds. Either the seconds per rep for one-sided and slow-tempo moves are too low, or the last segment needs its share of the change time.
- **Pair seconds against the dose (c40).** Eleven reps of each of two movements, one done per side, plus 90 s of rest doesn't fit 176 to 209 s. Experienced bodyweight reps of eleven are also light for a targeted session with no load. Tempo is the only progression, and the dose rules could print it.
- **Circuit rest in round one (c41).** The 45 to 47 s between rounds is eaten by first-round setup talk. That is minor and the script handles it.

**The methods**
- **Paired over straight sets.** The rule worked for c38 (two rows, a deadlift, a squat and a press in 30 minutes, with no overhead work). It hurt c40: a glutes target got a dead bug pair because straight sets "would drop" patterns that a targeted session needn't cover. For a targeted goal, the patterns the rule protects should come from the target's own pool.
- **The circuit should group stations by position when getting up and down is costly.** For c41, floor, bench and floor gave sixteen transitions. Ordering the floor moves together and the bench moves together would make two transitions a round, which matters most when a limit makes every transition delicate. It would help any circuit with floor work.
- **The intervals fallback for advanced conditioning** in a bare room under a delicate low back is still a strength circuit (c36). That is a catalog limit more than a method fault.
- **Flow for mobility** now fits 30 minutes in a full gym (c39).
