## Easy adjustments

1. **Write down the "light" rule for a delicate limit** in `knowledge/programming.md`, and from there in the brief's guide. A movement that loads a delicate area at 1 uses a weight for twelve to fifteen reps with three or more left, whatever the level's rep range. Its self-check offers only lighter or steady, and the harder work goes to movements that don't touch the limit. Helps c03, c05 and c09, and every delicate-limit case.
2. **Let neutral-spine core survive a low-back limit.** Rate the dead bug, bench dead bug and pallof press at low-back 0 in `knowledge/movements.toml`, as the playbook already says "neutral-spine core work continues". Helps c09 and every case for listener c, whose standing limit is the low back.
3. **Rate every overhead-loaded movement neck 2**, as the playbook claims they are: the thruster, the overhead carry and the overhead triceps extension. A delicate neck then leaves them off the menu. Helps c05, c06 and every case for listener b.
4. **Tell writers the short-name rule in the brief's "Writing it"**: after a movement's full name in a segment, its last words ("split squats", "deadlift") pass the check, so the full name needn't be repeated. Helps c05, c06 and c08, and removes stiff repetition everywhere.
5. **Size the talk for long sessions in the brief.** Next to "Talk to time", give the session's approximate word total, and have the content plan add reserve pieces and a serial for sessions of thirty-five minutes or more, so the rests don't fall back on padding. Helps c06, c08 and c09 (all filled rests with coach talk), and every long case.

## What the scores say

- **The stories.** Rests were worth hearing when a find was split across two or more breaks with a promise ("I'll tell you why after round two"), when a quiz or myth was asked and answered later, when an open thread came back (c37's owl, c38's tram take), and when a line was woven into the coaching (c38's "piano piano" on every lowering). Every writer used the `[story]` tags cleanly, and none told a story during a hard set. Two writers slipped in a small fact of their own (c36's garlic, c39's cast iron). Coaching never crowded the stories out. The opposite risk showed in the mobility flows (c37, c39), where nearly every hold carries a story and the hold timing gets loose.
- **The catalog and the menu.** The bigger catalog served every kit. No writer was short of movements, and c39's writer called the menu "large and clear". ## Easy adjustments

1. **Give each limit cue a short form, and let the build accept it.** Say the full sentence the first time a movement touches the limit, then a few words ("head in line", "right foot resting") after. This stops a long cue landing in the middle of a set and lifts warmth and adherence. Helps c36, c38, c41.
2. **Add a getting-down line to the low-back cues** (and to any limit whose cues cover only getting up), such as "To get down, a hand on a chair or bench, one knee, then the other, then sit and roll to your back." Helps c36, c39.
3. **Name the floor pulls in the hotel-room kit note:** "If the door can't be trusted for a towel row, the prone Y-T-W raise or prone lat pulldown fills the pull." One sentence in `knowledge/kits.md` and its brief note. Helps c36, c40.
4. **Put a loads line in the brief when no weights are on record:** "No loads on hand: set each load by reps, e.g. a weight you could lift about twelve times for a set of ten." Helps c38, c41 (and any hotel-gym or first session).
5. **Fix the dead bug's timing in the catalog** to about five or six seconds a rep, to match its own "three out, three back" cue, so the lint's segment times hold. Helps c36, c40, c41.
