- **A clinician's rule beats the general stop rule** (c06, and c34's writer had to decide it): the brief's clinician-rules line and `knowledge/script.md` say that where a clinician rules out pain in an area, the two-or-three-out-of-ten allowance never applies there.
- **Carries load the neck lightly** (c32): `farmer-carry` and `suitcase-carry` rate neck 1, so a delicate neck still allows them, the brief marks the load, and an off-limits neck rules them out.
- **Nothing standing includes fetching the kit** (c41): under a limit that rules out standing, the brief and `knowledge/script.md` say to have everything set out within reach before the work starts.
- **A limit cue beats a movement's own setup** (c30's wide squat stance, c41's planted feet): `knowledge/script.md` says to adapt the setup out loud or choose another movement.
- **Step jacks rate groin 2** (c30): a side step is the playbook's test for loosening a groin limit, so a delicate groin keeps them out.

### What the scores say

**The catalog and the menu.**
- Nothing was missing: each brief's menu held a sound session for its kit, level and limits, and
  every declared movement checks out against the catalog's loads (no neck or calf load in c06 or c20;
  the fast-flagged cadence surge loads no neck, so a delicate neck allows it).
- The limit cues are per area, so the writers turned them into movement-specific cues themselves:
  the right heel on a bike pedal, the row's back foot, getting to and from the floor and the supine
  twist's head (c06), the head under a pulldown bar (c20). The menu's "load on today's limits" prints
  only nonzero loads, so a movement that touches a limit without loading it carries no note: the
  goblet squat (ankle 2, calf 0) under a delicate calf, where the writer still judged depth by the
  calf, and the lat pulldown (overhead flag, neck 0) under a delicate neck.
- "Easier" and "Harder" lines name movements that aren't on today's menu (split squat under reverse
  lunge and the Bulgarian split squat in c06), which the lint would refuse if spoken.
- `towel-door-row` needs only a towel but depends on a solid inside door that opens away; on a
  hotel-room kit it is the only pull, and the brief names no fallback (c08).
- The lint matched "easy rows" to the rowing machine's alias (c08's notes), a false positive.
- No brief carried loads on hand, so every weight was cued by feel; fine for invented listeners, but
  the progression lines ("next pair up") have nothing to anchor to.

**The programming guide.**
- Two dose rules collide on a hotel rack: experienced strength reps of 6 to 10, and 15 to 30 with
  light dumbbells. The guide doesn't say which wins (c06 kept 8 to 10 and used tempo).
- The hard-set cap (10 to 16 at the experienced level) and the house arithmetic ("forty-five about
  twenty") disagree for long sessions. c06 and c08 both landed on exactly 16.
- The general pain rule (two or three out of ten) and a stricter clinician rule for one area need an
  explicit order in `knowledge/script.md`: the clinician's rule replaces the general one for its area.
- Two equipment notes don't fit the limits or the lint: the dumbbell note's "bring them to the chest
  and roll up" invites loaded neck flexion under a delicate neck (c20 flagged it and cued "the legs
  rock you upright, not a crunch of the chin"; c06 used the same incline but only "rock up"), and the
  full-gym note's "name a fallback for every main movement" collides with the menu check on undeclared
  names (c20 worded around it).
- The writers read the rest of the guide well: rehearsal sets as the warm-up's second half, the last
  set of a simple movement near failure only where no limit applies (c08's push-ups), the capped-load
  order (more reps, slower lowering, pause, then the next pair) said in the audio, and one change at a
  time.

**The design.**
- All three chose paired sets: two because the last session was a circuit (as the guide suggests),
  and c08 for a first session in a small room. Each choice served the case, with upper/lower pairs,
  multi-joint work first and core last.
- The week drove the design: the hinge went first in c06 and c20 as the untrained pattern, and c20
  filled four gaps (hinge, vertical pull, carry, anti-rotation) in thirty minutes, at the cost of
  leaving the quads to the bike.
- c20 used time best: the welcome and rules ride on the bike warm-up, zones are grouped with timed
  walks, and the finisher is short and inside the limit. c06 spent about 590 s before the first working
  set and dropped the finisher, so its 45 minutes lean on talk.
- The one safety slip in these three is in the writing, not the design: the plans themselves respect
  every limit.

### Changes

Run 4's rows were c40 5, 3, 4, 3 and c41 4, 4, 3, 3 (safety, efficacy, adherence, ease). The old warmth scores are the ones in the table above. Totals over the four shared scores: c40 went from 15 to 17, and c41 from 14 to 13. With warmth: c40 went from 18 to 21, and c41 from 16 to 17.

- **c40:**
  - **Efficacy 3 to 4 (design).** Run 4's skeleton applied a paired-sets rule that put air squats and dead bugs in pair one of a glutes session. Free of it, the writer gave each pair a glute movement (Bulgarian split squat, single-leg bridge) and closed with a liked bridge set near failure. It lacks abduction work, which is what holds it below 5.
  - **Ease 3 to 4 (brief).** Before 7.0.0, ease measured invention beyond a skeleton whose set seconds couldn't hold its doses. It now measures how much the brief supplied, and the writer set its own segment times. The brief left only "the far handle" and a pairing rest that one-sided pairs can't keep.
  - **Warmth 3 to 4 (script and brief).** The brief's name line ("Sam ... two to four times a session") and the writer's own choices replace "Minute N. Pair X, set Y" and the counted reps: varied openings, why lines fitted to a traveller, and a close that names the next step.
  - Safety held at 5, and the earlier script's high knees over a hotel floor are gone.
- **c41:**
  - **Efficacy 4 to 3 (design).** Run 4's circuit had four rounds of six stations: a heavier pair for the bench press and rows, a lighter pair overhead, and four sets of the healthy-side bridge. This writer chose one pair, sized for the overhead press, to save steps on the ankle. That leaves the rows and the floor press light, and the healthy leg gets one set.
  - **Adherence 3 to 4 (design).** All the bench work comes before the floor, so there are two transitions where run 4's skeleton made sixteen. The blocks also vary: bench pairs, seated intervals, floor pairs, hips.
  - **Ease 3 to 2 (brief, partly calibration).** Both scores record heavy invention: the fetch, the foot at every station, the transitions. But run 4 scored it against a skeleton that had already picked the movements. Now the writer also had to screen the menu by its setup text.
    - Run 4's own load fixes landed: the push-up, the glute bridge and the supine twist now carry ankle and foot loads, so the bridge left the menu and the twist went to healthy side only.
    - Its findings on kneeling moves are still open: cat-cow, thread the needle and bird dog carry no ankle or foot load. So is foot drive in the dumbbell bench press, and coaching notes still have no limit variants.
    - 6.2.0's limit cues now cover the moves down to the floor and back up.
  - **Warmth 2 to 4 (script and brief).** Against run 4's run of instructions: a first-session welcome by name, plain why lines, and a close that asks how the foot feels tomorrow.
  - **Safety held at 4, for a different fault.** Run 4's sixteen moves between floor and bench are gone. What remains is the walk to the rack and back with dumbbells. Run 4's writer scripted the same walk ("walk them over the way you walk day to day"), and run 4's judge counted it as invention rather than a safety cost. The brief still says nothing about fetching kit when an ankle is off limits.
