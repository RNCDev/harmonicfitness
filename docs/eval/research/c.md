## Cooking / explainer: Why a dry surface browns and a wet one steams

The reason behind that crust, for a set's worth of thinking.

Browning is a reaction between proteins and sugars that only gets going above about 140 degrees Celsius. Water can't get hotter than 100 while it's boiling off. So as long as the surface of a steak or a mushroom is wet, the pan's heat goes into turning that water into steam, and the food goes grey instead of brown.

That's why cooks pat meat dry, salt it ahead so the surface dries in the fridge, and don't crowd the pan: crowded food releases water faster than it can evaporate, and everything steams. Give each piece room and the surface dries in seconds, then browns.

## - / brain_teaser: The two candles

A puzzle short enough for one rest.

You have two candles. Each burns for exactly one hour, but unevenly, so half the length isn't half the time. With a lighter and nothing else, how do you time exactly forty-five minutes?

Answer: Light the first candle at both ends and the second at one end. The first burns out in thirty minutes, since two flames meet halfway in time. At that moment, light the other end of the second candle: its remaining thirty minutes burn in fifteen. Thirty plus fifteen is forty-five.
