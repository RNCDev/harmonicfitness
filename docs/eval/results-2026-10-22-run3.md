# Eval results: the twelve conditioning cases, third judged run

- **Date:** 2026-10-22, after the second run (`results-2026-10-22-run2.md`) and the 5.0.0 release.
- **What changed before this run:** intervals work a timed movement for its own seconds (a twenty-second sprint rests the rest of the interval); when the strength fallback joins the intervals and nothing is on a machine, the block runs as a circuit, one interval of each movement a round; and scripts can mark timed work with `[hold]`, which keeps a segment its full length with its spare time silent at the hold, so a short effort no longer has to be talked through.
- **How the run was done:** the twelve conditioning cases (c25 to c36, all intervals) were rewritten by three writers from `docs/eval/writer.md` and scored blind by two judges on `docs/eval/judge.md`, who then compared with the second run's forty-case table and scripts. Only these twelve were rerun; strength, general, mobility and targeted cases are as the second run left them.
- **Caveat:** two judges, not four, so calibration may differ from the second run.

## Fixed after this run, not scored again

- **`[hold 20s]`:** every writer had to balance word counts to place the bells, because bare holds share the spare time equally (c26, c28 to c30, c32 to c36). A hold with seconds is now exactly that much silence, with no ten-second cap, and the bare holds take what's left, so a script times the work with a fixed hold and gives the rest one bare hold.
- **Hard blocks stay hard:** above novice, an interval block repeats the strongest hard movement before a moderate one leads it (c31 to c35 had a steady ride or incline walk as a hard block).
- **The steady block goes on a machine,** even one an interval already used, before floor work (c28's ten minutes of step jacks in a gym with a bike).

## Still open

- The lint could print each `[chime]`'s predicted time against the skeleton's on and off seconds.
- Remainder seconds land on whichever slot holds them (round four of c30 and c36; block ends in c34); they could be one labelled transition.
- The fallback circuit gives strength moves conditioning rests (35 on, 45 off for bridges); a one-sided station gets one interval for both legs (c36).
- Machine intervals have no cue for what "on" and "off" mean on that machine (incline walk, elliptical), and the recovery activity is never named (c25 to c29).
- Advanced hotel-room conditioning has only high knees as a hard move once jumping jacks are disliked: burpees, squat jumps, skaters and mountain climbers are absent or drafts (c35, c36).
- A delicate limit that rules out fast work could mark the slot "controlled tempo", so the catalog's "brisk" cue doesn't need overriding (c27, c30).
