# Eval results: the session builder's grid, second judged run

- **Date:** 2026-10-22, the same day as the first run (`results-2026-10-22.md`), after the 4.0.0 fix wave.
- **Catalog commit:** 5098c2e (release 4.0.0).
- **Methods chosen in this run:** circuit (14 cases), intervals (12), straight-sets (7), paired-sets (5), flow (2). In the first run: circuit 16, straight-sets 10, paired-sets 10, intervals 2, flow 2. Every conditioning case moved from circuit to intervals, and most general cases from straight or paired sets to circuit.
- **How the run was done:** `docs/eval/skeletons.sh` regenerated the forty skeletons. Eight writers, five cases each, wrote the scripts from `docs/eval/writer.md` (first candidate, the skeleton's doses, every deviation logged in `<id>.writer-notes.md`), with no research: topic beats are one-line placeholders. All forty lint clean within three percent. Four judges, ten cases each in the first run's partitions, scored blind on `docs/eval/judge.md`, then read the first run's rows and scripts and labelled each moved score as skeleton, script or calibration.
- **Caveats:** each judge saw only their ten cases, so calibration may differ between judges, as in the first run. Two of c16's and c18's sets were counted out by a writer's helper script (one line per rep), which costs those two adherence points in the script, not the skeleton.

What moved, by the judges' labels:

- **Efficacy and adherence rose, mostly from the skeleton.** Level ranking put loaded and level-matched lifts first (c05, c06, c09 to c12, c19, c20), the method choice by goal fit gave general sessions circuits that hold their rest (c14, c15, c17, c18), and conditioning moved off two-minute strength rests onto intervals (c33 efficacy 1 to 4, c35 1 to 3, c29 2 to 3). The rest came from the scripts: counted sets instead of five-second set calls, no rest talk inside circuits, no boilerplate filler (c03, c04, c07, c19, c20).
- **Ease fell in 20 of 40 cases (and rose in 3)**, almost all from the skeleton: the newly printed warm-up and finisher lists put sprints and climbs first and overlapped the work (c05, c06, c09 to c12, c19 to c22, c25 to c28, c31, c34, c38, c39); interval slots put marching or an easy spin first as the "hard" effort (c26, c27, c31, c32, c35, c36); paired sets still had one marker per movement and slots too short for the printed rest (c03, c05, c09, c10, c12); air squat still led goblet squat with dumbbells on hand (c05, c06, c19, c20); the finisher had no dose (c02, c04, c06, c08, c10, c12, c20, c38, c40).
- **Conditioning barely moved (13.33 to 13.42) because the fix wave traded one fault for another.** Intervals use locomotion only, so a thin kit or a delicate limit left marching: c36 (advanced, hotel room, low back delicate) fell from 14 to 8 with thirty rounds of marching, c30 (groin delicate) from 15 to 10 with twenty-one.

## Implemented for 5.0.0

Each item names the finding it answers.

1. **Effort on conditioning entries** (catalog format 3): `effort = "easy" | "moderate" | "hard"` on every finished conditioning movement, checked by `catalog.problems`. Interval work never takes an easy movement; above novice hard ranks first, at novice moderate.
2. **Interval fallback:** when fewer than two locomotion movements are hard enough today, the intervals block adds timed squat, lunge, hinge and core work (`fallback` in `sessions.toml`), with a warning. c30 and c36 now run step jacks, bridges, dead bugs, squats and split squats.
3. **Caps:** `hard_sets_per_session` holds for straight and paired sets (circuit stations are lighter and are not capped); `efforts_per_session` caps intervals, and time past it goes to a steady block of a moderate movement. Interval rounds per movement start lower (three or four), so a capped session holds more movements.
4. **Warm-up, finisher and cool-down lists:** the warm-up takes easy locomotion, then moderate locomotion with no fast or impact work, then mobility, the first work movement's equipment first; the finisher takes hard, then moderate conditioning, with a printed dose from the level's conditioning table (`Finisher: 240 s, 4 rounds of 30 s on, 30 s off`); the cool-down leads with box breathing. None leads with a movement the work uses, and the finisher doesn't lead with the warm-up's first.
5. **Paired sets get one marker per pair set** ("Pair 1 of 3, set 2 of 3: goblet squat, then floor press"), and pairing picks the partner family most of the rest share, so no like pair is left at the end.
6. **Printed rests are the rests the slots hold:** straight sets print the rest their seconds hold, a circuit prints the rest between rounds on its block line and in each round's marker ("then rest 60 s"), and paired sets print "rest N s after each pair set".
7. **Ranking by kit load:** with load on hand, a strength movement that uses it ranks ahead of a bodyweight one at the same level; with a cardio machine on hand, conditioning on it ranks ahead of conditioning on the floor.
8. **Bodyweight reps above novice** (`bodyweight_reps`, 10 to 15 experienced, 12 to 20 advanced), applied to any movement that needs no load equipment.
9. **Circuit rest between rounds** of 45 to 90 s, the method's own, in place of the strength dose's; the circuit's patterns add vertical push, vertical pull and anti-rotation, so a long circuit's second pass comes later.
10. **Targeted sessions give each target pattern two slots**, and a muscle target picks the patterns whose movements list it among their first two muscles.
11. **Intervals:** one machine's slots together, thirty seconds to change equipment, markers numbered by block ("block 2 of 4, interval 3 of 4", "then move to the elliptical"), and no movement repeated while another remains.
12. **Safety:** a low back off limits also rules out `spine-flexion` and `spine-twist`; the overhead presses rate neck 2, so a delicate neck rules them out; `face-pull` rates wrist 1. Both are written into the playbook.
13. **Limits and sides:** a case `--limit` prints what it means ("light loads only on the left calf, no impact or fast work"); a healthy-side-only slot is timed for one side; patterns holding a liked movement come right after the targets.
14. **Catalog:** step jacks, air bike steady and sprint, stair climber, standing climb, leg extension, arm circles, shoulder rolls, leg swings and the standing calf stretch finished; the towel door row's setup rewritten ("an inside door that opens away from you, never the room's entry door"); the world's greatest stretch keeps the head in line.
15. **Flow** runs two mobility drills to each breathing one.
16. **Private movements:** `me/movements.toml` is now loaded (it was ignored), and `./sweatcast check` lists any problem in it.
17. **Harness:** `docs/eval/writer.md` is the writers' brief; `judge.md` reads the writer's notes, scores adherence on the coaching alone, and compares with an earlier run after scoring blind; the README names same-day runs with `-run2` and keeps the table's columns; the lint's exports into the listeners' `workouts/` are git-ignored.
