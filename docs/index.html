The big picture

How it fits together

A request for a workout becomes a narrated MP3 for earbuds. Four folders make that happen. Three hold what the app knows, and one, tools/, does the work.

The map

Shared rules, a coach and one person's details all feed the engine. It turns them into a checked script and then audio, and saves what happened back to that person's folder.

knowledge/

What's true for everyone

The movement catalog, the session methods and doses, the rules for safe scripts and for what a voice can read, the story formats and the voice services.

Gives: the safe menu, the doses and the rules every draft is checked against.
coaches/

Who speaks

Each coach is a persona (a name and how they talk) with a voice. Local coaches speak on this computer; web coaches speak through a voice service. A listener's own coaches live in me/coaches/.

Gives: the character the script is written in, its pace, and the voice that reads it.
me/

One listener

Private, and never in the engine's git. Who they are, their kit and weights, their injuries, their topics, their log and their keys.

Gives: today's limits, the kit, the week so far and what to talk about through the session.

The engine

Every step runs through one command, so one permission covers it.

  1. 01

    Ask

    A short check-in: anything sore, the goal, the length, the kit, the coach.

  2. 02

    Brief

    The ingredients: who they are, today's limits and the safe menu of movements.

  3. 03

    Write

    The AI writer plans the session and writes the coaching and the stories.

  4. 04

    Check

    The build lints the draft: menu, timing, silences and what a voice can say.

  5. 05

    Speak

    The coach's voice reads every line, and the render lays it on the clock.

  6. 06

    Deliver

    The MP3 goes to their phone's folder (or the web app), and the session is logged.

Back into me/: the draft, the MP3's record, the log line, the stories told and the threads left open. The next workout reads them.

What each step reads and writes

A solid chip is read and a dashed one is written.

  1. Check-in.

    A few quick questions, and only the ones the request didn't already answer. A lasting change to an injury goes into the health file, with permission.

    profile, training, health, log health (only if it changed)
  2. Brief.

    Applies today's limits to the movement catalog and keeps only what is safe for this kit and level. The level also says how much the coach explains, from a full walk-through for someone new to one cue for someone who knows the moves. It saves a brief and a menu beside the draft. The build later refuses anything the menu doesn't hold.

    catalog, sessions, programming character, pace profile, health, log workouts/<day>/ brief + menu
  3. Write.

    The plan becomes segments, each with its seconds, then the words: the coaching first, sized to each segment's time, then the stories for the steady timed work and the rests, from that day's research. On the website the clock comes first as a skeleton, whose lint gives each segment a word budget.

    script + voice rules, content formats topics, research the draft
  4. Build: check.

    One command checks the draft: only menu movements, a one-sided limit's side named in the opening, no silence over eight seconds, the total within five percent of plan, and only words a voice can read.

    catalog pace menu
  5. Build: speak.

    A local coach renders on this computer, and a speech recognizer checks each take. A web coach sends the script to its service, using the listener's key.

    voice keys (engine only) MP3, build record
  6. Deliver and log.

    The MP3 is copied to the delivery folder. Then the session, the stories told and any open threads are logged, and me/ is backed up to its own private repo.

    log, told, threads, backup

How a script gets written

Both routes share the brief, the menu and the build's checks. Only the writing differs.

On a computer · Claude Code or Codex

  1. Research starts in the background

    while the check-in is answered.

  2. Brief, then a plan.

    A small table of blocks, movements, doses and seconds.

  3. Coaching first.

    Every segment's setup, cues and holds. A local coach can render ahead as the writing lands.

  4. Stories once research reports,

    each wrapped in [story] tags, in the steady work and the rests.

  5. Read it through and rewrite it as one piece.
  6. Lint, fix, repeat, then build.

On the website · a writers' room

  1. The agent writes the skeleton.

    Each set's label carries its dose and weight, each rest is a segment of its own, and a steady timed set that will carry a story is marked. Its lint returns each segment's word budget, and once it's clean the writers start at once.

  2. Researcher

    starts as soon as the brief is saved. It researches, then returns each story as notes: why it's theirs, the facts, the point. Only a source from the past two weeks makes something news.

  3. Trainer,

    at the same time, returns notes for each segment: the setup, the cues, the weight, and the safety points, which the host says in the coach's own words.

  4. Host,

    the coach's voice, writes every word from both, as one person talking. A rest's story gets most of its time.

  5. Trainer's review

    checks the opening's rules and the sides, and fixes what the build's check names, leaving the voice alone (up to twice).

  6. The build prices it.

    The listener presses Record, and the same build records. A workout asked for at a later time is checked in at once and scheduled with a ceiling; all of this runs fifteen minutes before it and records on its own.

Rules that always hold

Only the menuEvery movement comes from today's safe menu. The build refuses anything else.
The right sideUnder a one-sided limit, the opening names the sore side, and a one-sided movement never speaks of the other.
No long silenceNo quiet over eight seconds (twenty with the pulse bed). With no screen, a silent gap sounds like a dropped earbud.
On timeThe total stays within five percent of plan. A natural voice matters more than exact segments.
Keys stay putOnly the engine reads me/.env, when it calls a voice service. No assistant reads it.
Private stays privateme/ never enters the engine's git. A hook and CI both check.
