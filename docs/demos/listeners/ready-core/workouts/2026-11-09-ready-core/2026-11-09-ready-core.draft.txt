@@ 70 | Welcome
I'm Priya, and this is thirty minutes for your middle. Not endless crunches. We're going to teach your core its real job, which is holding you steady while your arms and legs move, and turning when you ask it to. I think about backs the way a physio does, so we'll do it well.

This workout was written by AI, not by a trainer or a clinician, and it's ready-made, the same for everyone, so it knows nothing about your body or any injury you carry. You're the expert there.

A mild ache, up to about two or three out of ten, is fine. If something hurts, skip it or ease off.

Sharp pain or new swelling tells you that movement isn't for today. Numbness, tingling, dizziness, or a joint that gives way tells you the session isn't, so stop there. And chest pain or pressure, feeling faint, or a heart that's racing or pounding means stop everything and get help.

You'll want a sturdy chair with its back to a wall, a towel, and a little floor space. Every movement has an easier and a harder way, and you'll hear both.

@@ 105 | Shadow boxing, round one
Now stand up, and we'll turn. This is shadow boxing, but slow, and braced. Feet a little wider than your hips, knees soft, hands up by your cheeks.

Reach one fist forward and across your body, and let your ribs and hip turn behind it. Let the back heel turn out a little as you turn. Pause there for a breath, then come back to the middle, and send the other fist. About one every three or four seconds. The work isn't in the arm, it's in the brace that steers the turn.

If that's easy, sink a little lower in the knees and turn a little further. If standing is hard, sit on the front of the chair and turn from there. [count 3 go]
Reach, turn, and pause. Feet stay planted.

[story] That turn from the middle is how a cat lands on its feet. In eighteen ninety-four a French scientist filmed a cat being dropped upside down. People thought it must push off the hand that let it go. His photographs showed it falling with no spin to start with, and still it turned over and landed on its feet. [/story]

Halfway. Hands back to your cheeks between punches.

[story] The trick is that a cat bends at the middle and turns its two halves separately. Front legs tucked in, the front half turns first. Then the front half holds still while the back half follows. Top half, bottom half, in turn. Rather like your ribs and hips right now. [/story]

[hold]
Last ten seconds, slow and braced. [hold 6s] And let the hands drop.

@@ 85 | Dead bug, set one
This is a dead bug, named for how it looks, like a beetle on its back. Your middle stays still while your arms and legs move, and that's the whole skill.

Lift your arms straight up over your shoulders. Lift your knees over your hips, shins level, like a table top. Breathe out, brace, and let your low back settle gently into the floor.

Reach one arm back past your head and the opposite leg out long, then bring them back, and swap. Each reach is one. If that's easy, reach the leg lower, closer to the floor. If your back arches, keep the reach shorter. Ten, five a side. [count 3 go] [reps 10]
