@@ 68 | Welcome
I'm Priya, and if you've sat all day, this one's for you. Thirty minutes to loosen your hips, your back and your shoulders, slowly, with nothing to rush.

So you know what you're listening to, this is a ready-made session, written by AI rather than a trainer or a clinician who knows you, and recorded the same for everyone. It isn't adapted to you or to any injury, so you're the expert on your own body today.

A mild ache, up to about a two or three out of ten, is fine. Anything that hurts more, skip it or ease off. Sharp pain or new swelling means that movement isn't for today. Numbness, tingling, dizziness or a joint giving way means we stop for today.

Chest pain or pressure, feeling faint, or a racing heart: stop altogether, and get help.

You'll want a sturdy chair with its back against a wall, a towel, and a little floor space in front of it. Every movement has a gentler way and a fuller way, and I'll offer both. Sit towards the front of the chair, and we'll begin right there.

@@ 108 | Seated twist
Now a seated twist, for the middle of your back, the part that stiffens most when you sit. Feet flat, knees pointing ahead. Breathe in and sit tall, then breathe out and turn your chest to the right, left hand on the outside of your right knee, right hand on the seat behind you.

Let your body set the range. The hands only steady you, they don't crank. If there's more there, grow taller on each breath in and turn a touch further on each breath out.

@@ 104 | Wall angel
Up out of the chair now, the hardest step. Stand with your back to the wall beside the chair, heels a short step out, and your seat, upper back and head touching the wall. Bend your elbows and rest the backs of your arms on the wall, hands at head height, like a goalpost.

This is the wall angel, for shoulders that spend the day rounded forward. Ribs down, slide your arms slowly up the wall, then draw the elbows back down. Only as high as they stay touching. If a shoulder pinches, keep the hands lower. If it's easy, step your heels closer to the wall. [count 3 go]

Slide up, breathing in. [pause 3s] Elbows down, breathing out. [pause 4s]

Ribs down, and the lower back easy against the wall. [pause 4s]

Up again, [pause 3s] and down, squeezing gently between the shoulder blades. [pause 4s]

Keep going at your own pace, about one every five seconds. [pause 5s]

Slide up, and breathe in. [pause 3s] Elbows down, and breathe out. [pause 4s]

Wherever your arms leave the wall, that's your range today. [pause 4s]
And let the arms drop.

Breathe in, belly drops. [pause 3s] Breathe out, and round. [pause 5s]

@@ 130 | Side-lying open book, then sphinx

Breathe out and open your top arm in a slow arc, across and towards the floor behind you, following your hand with your eyes, then bring it back to meet the other hand. Knees stay stacked. If the shoulder complains, bend the elbow and open only halfway. Five on this side. [count 3 go] [reps 5]
Breathe out as you open. [hold 3s]
Knees together, and back. [hold 3s]
Last one, back to meet the other hand.

Now roll onto your back, and over onto your right side. [pause 4s] Knees stacked and high, arms out in front, palms together. [pause 3s] Five more. [cue go] [reps 5]
Open, and breathe out. [hold 3s]
Only as far as is comfortable. [hold 3s]
Last one, and all the way back. [cue rest]

Roll onto your front, and prop yourself up on your forearms, elbows under your shoulders, hips and belly heavy on the floor. This is the sphinx, the gentle opposite of sitting hunched. @@ 117 | Cool-down, ninety-ninety breathing
Last of all, and my favourite. Shuffle round so your seat is near the chair, and rest your calves on its seat, so your hips and knees are both bent at about a right angle. If that's awkward, keep your feet flat on the floor instead. One hand on your chest, one on your belly.

This is ninety-ninety breathing. Breathe in through the nose and let the belly rise under your hand, then breathe out slowly and let everything soften. If you'd like more, make each breath out a little longer than the last.
