## Birds / profile: The Godwit That Flew Eleven Days Without Landing

Hook: Ines, while you breathe slowly, picture a bird five months old, with no map and no rest stop, simply keeping on.

- A bar-tailed godwit is a long-legged shorebird. In 2022 researchers followed one young godwit, about five months old, by satellite. It left the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta in southwest Alaska and flew to Ansons Bay, in north-eastern Tasmania. It flew eleven days, without landing once.
- The distance was at least 13,560 kilometres, about 8,435 miles, roughly two thousand kilometres farther than the earlier famous flight of a godwit called E7, which went from Alaska to New Zealand in 2007. BirdLife Australia describes this young bird's flight as a world record for nonstop flight. The team had tagged it as a hatchling.
- Before leaving, a godwit rebuilds its body. USGS scientists, Theunis Piersma and Robert Gill among them, found that departing godwits carry fat as more than half their body mass, and that their gizzards, livers, kidneys and guts shrink. They found these changes happen within about a month. It sheds the parts it will not need, and carries fuel.

## Gardening / explainer: Why Young Sunflowers Follow the Sun, and Grown Ones Stop

Hook: Ines, a slow turn toward the light is something a sunflower does too, and it does it with a clock inside its stem.

- Young sunflowers face east at dawn, then turn slowly west through the day, following the sun. At night they swing back toward the east, ready for morning. This is called heliotropism, and researchers published the explanation in the journal Science in 2016, led by Stacey Harmer at UC Davis.
- The turning is growth. The east side of the stem grows faster during the day, bending the plant west, and the west side grows faster at night, bending it back. The plant's circadian clock, a cycle of about twenty-four hours, times it. Plants held still or turned away ended with less leaf area and less biomass.
- When the flower matures, growth slows and the movement stops, leaving the head facing east. East-facing blooms warm faster in the morning, and bees favour warm flowers: they drew about five times as many pollinators as west-facing ones. The plant stops chasing the sun, and waits for it.

## Birds / myth_or_fact: Will Parent Birds Abandon a Chick You Touched?

Hook: Ines, a gentle touch is not the end of the story, for a chick or for anyone easing back into things.

- The belief says a baby bird touched by a person will be rejected by its parents. It is a myth. Most birds have a poor sense of smell, and they recognise their young by sight and sound, much as people do. The RSPB and Audubon both say handling a young bird will not make its parents abandon it.
- Parents are devoted and are not easily put off. A fledgling on the ground is often not orphaned: it is meant to hop about while still fed, and the parents are usually hiding nearby. The RSPB advises leaving healthy fledglings alone.
- The real risk is disturbance. Hovering near a nest can draw predators, and long visits can unsettle the parents. If a bird is in danger, the RSPB says to move it only a short distance, within hearing of where it was found. Lingering is the harm, not touching.
