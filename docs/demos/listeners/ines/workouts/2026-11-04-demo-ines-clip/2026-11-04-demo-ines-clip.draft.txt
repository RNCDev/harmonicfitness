@@ 75 | The last flow, pull-aparts with the breath
Last flow, Ines, and this one belongs to the breath. Same pull-apart, only slower: breathe out as you open the band to your chest, and in as it comes back. Breathing out as you open keeps the ribs down, and that keeps your back quiet. [count 3 go]

Out, and open. [pause 2s] In, and back. [pause 2s]

[story] And here's the bird I promised. The bar-tailed godwit is a long-legged shorebird, and researchers once followed a young one by satellite, only about five months old. It left Alaska and flew to Tasmania.

Eleven days, without landing once, at least thirteen thousand five hundred kilometres. It was reported as a record. Five months old. [/story]

Breathe out, and open. [pause 2s]

[story] What gets me is how it got ready. Before a godwit leaves, it rebuilds its own body. Fat becomes more than half its weight, and the organs it won't need on the way, the gut among them, shrink. It packs fuel, and sheds what it can't carry. [/story]

[hold]
Last one, slowly all the way back. [cue stop]

@@ 45 | Cool-down, long exhale breathing
Let the band go, and sit on the mat, cross-legged or kneeling, hands in your lap.

[story] Here's what I take from that bird. The hard part happened before it ever left the ground. It got ready, and then it simply kept going. [/story] Today was a little of that for your back.

In through the nose for four. [pause 4s] Out through the mouth for six. [pause 6s]

Once more. In for four. [pause 4s] And out for six, soft, like fogging a mirror. [pause 6s]

@@ 30 | Close
That's the session, Ines. Thirty-five minutes: your spine moved every way it likes to, your hips opened, and the band woke up your upper back. Your lower back should feel warmer and looser now, and your breathing slower. When you get up, roll to your side first.
