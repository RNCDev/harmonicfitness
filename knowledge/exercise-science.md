# Exercise science facts

Verified facts for the science line tied to the movement happening that minute, kept here so no session has to look them up again. One entry per movement, with its source and what the fact doesn't show. Research adds at most two movements a session. A movement with no entry gets a form cue instead of a fact.

- **Dead bug:** Sternlicht and colleagues (2001) found the "dying bug" mostly worked the abdominal muscles, while a quadruped exercise worked the trunk and hip extensors more; no muscle went above 41 percent of its maximum contraction. A 2017 study of 30 adults found the abs worked harder at faster speeds, and when arms and legs moved together rather than one pair at a time. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11689975/
- **Side plank:** In the full side bridge, gluteus medius activity is high (74 percent of maximum), gluteus maximus low (21 percent) and external oblique substantial (69 percent). There's no data for the version from the knee, so don't present these numbers as being about it. Source: https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/29592-the-reliability-and-validity-of-gluteal-endurance-measures-gems
- **Rows:** Fenwick and colleagues (2009, Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research) compared three rows. The standing bent-over row worked the back hard but put the largest load on the lumbar spine; the inverted row had the lowest erector spinae activity and spine load. Chest-supported and seated rows weren't tested, so the low-back case for them is reasoning, not data. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19197209/
- **Clamshell:** Selkowitz, Beneck and Powers (2013, Journal of Orthopaedic and Sports Physical Therapy) used fine-wire EMG on 20 adults doing 11 exercises. The clam had the highest ratio of glute work to tensor fasciae latae work (a muscle at the front of the hip), ahead of the sidestep, single-leg bridge and quadruped hip extension. A later study disagrees. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6088126
- **Box breathing:** Balban and colleagues (Stanford, Cell Reports Medicine, January 10, 2023) ran a month-long randomized trial of five minutes a day. Breathwork beat mindfulness meditation for mood and lowering breathing rate, and exhale-focused cyclic sighing did best. Box breathing wasn't singled out as beating meditation. Source: https://hubermanlab.stanford.edu/publications/brief-structured-respiration-practices-enhance-mood-and-reduce-physiological-arousal
- **Single-leg glute bridge:** A 2023 study of 64 young women (European Journal of Investigation in Health, Psychology and Education) found the banded single-leg glute bridge worked the hip muscles at 90 percent of maximum (give or take 28) versus 70 percent (give or take 15) for the turn-out bent knee pulse. It was a banded bridge, not a plain one, and it wasn't compared with the two-leg bridge. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10529521/
- **Prone hip extension:** Lee and colleagues (2019, Journal of Physical Therapy Science, 26 healthy adults, average age 23) compared a plain prone hip lift to 10 degrees with one done with the hip turned out 30 degrees and the knee bent 90 degrees. The plain lift worked the hamstring (biceps femoris) at 56 percent of maximum and the glute max at 15 percent; the bent-knee, turned-out version flipped it to glute max 39 percent and hamstring 14 percent. Five-second holds, surface EMG, open-chain only; it doesn't show what happens over a set of reps or in standing movements. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6382482

Searched without a confirmed study (don't search again): dumbbell floor press, straight-leg raise, seated hockey stickhandling, incline push-up (only a weak 2024 bar-elevated study found), suitcase carry or hold for the lateral core (the one 2024 study, International Journal of Exercise Science 17(1), measured external oblique and back muscles but not the quadratus lumborum, with 18 usable participants; the quadratus lumborum claim rests on McGill's non-EMG summaries).
