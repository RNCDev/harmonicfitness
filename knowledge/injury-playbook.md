# Injury playbook: the evidence

Working around a known, assessed injury; not a substitute for a clinician. **A clinician's rules always win**: write them word for word under `## Clinician rules` in `me/health.md`. Where this file, `knowledge/limits.toml`, `knowledge/kits.md` and `me/health.md` disagree, the strictest wins.

This file is the evidence and the reasoning. What each limit means for a session, for the check-in and after it clears is in `knowledge/limits.toml`, which the tools read: the brief quotes today's level for each limit, and `./harmonicfitness get limits` gives the check-in its signs. Each area below says which of its lines there are sourced and which are house judgment; no source gives pass marks for moving on, so every loosening sign is house judgment. The research notes behind the 2026-11-02 additions are in `knowledge/research/` (red flags; neck and hip; elbow, wrist and rib; Achilles, knee and pain monitoring), with what each couldn't confirm.

## Pain during exercise
The stop rule in `knowledge/script.md` (pain up to two or three out of ten; sharp pain or new swelling stops the movement; numbness, tingling, dizziness or giving way stops the session) is **house judgment, deliberately stricter than the sources**: the trials allowed pain up to five out of ten during exercise, settling by the next morning. The stricter cap suits someone training alone with only a voice in their ear. Two areas go stricter still, from their own sources: the elbow (no flare during or after) and the rib (nothing that worsens the pain, said as no worse than when you started, since a bruised rib can ache at rest). The next-morning rule is sourced. Also house judgment: which signs end a movement and which end the session (`stop` and `stop_today` in limits.toml, one list per sign so it means the same everywhere); pain spreading down a limb from the back or neck, a headache that builds with a sore neck and a limp coming on all end the session, and under an area off limits or a delicate rib, pain there that's still worse after a short rest ends it too (as does a rib's catch on an easy breath that won't settle), while the back off limits takes "no worse than when you started" in place of two or three out of ten.
- A meta-analysis of 7 small, unblinded trials (385 people with chronic musculoskeletal pain) found exercise allowed into pain gave a small short-term benefit over pain-free exercise and no difference later; the trials allowed up to 5 out of 10 (or 50 mm on a 100 mm scale), with pain to settle by the next session or morning. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5739826/
- Continuing activity under a pain-monitoring model did no harm against six weeks' rest in Achilles tendinopathy (38 patients; the abstract gives no pain numbers). Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17307888/

## Red flags
The lists are in `knowledge/limits.toml`: `red_flags` (stop, and see a clinician soon) and `emergency` (call emergency services now), each in `[general]` and under the areas that have their own. They stop the session before it starts and are said in chat, never in the audio. The joint signs in `[general] red_flags` (a joint that gives way or locks, the knee's included) are house judgment; the rest are sourced:
- Heart: chest pain, pressure or tightness (999 for tight or squeezing chest pain), fainting, a racing or irregular heartbeat with chest pain, breathlessness or feeling faint (999), dizziness that doesn't settle when you stop, and breathlessness suddenly getting worse (111). "Breathlessness out of proportion to the effort" has no source and isn't used. Sources: https://eparmedx.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/PARQPlus2025Fillable.pdf (PAR-Q+), https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000094.htm , https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/symptoms/ , https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fainting/ , https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-palpitations/
- A clot in the leg: throbbing pain or swelling in one calf or thigh, red or darkened skin (urgent: 111); with breathlessness or chest pain, 999. Both are in `[general]`, so every check-in carries them, not only one with a calf limit. Warmth is from MedlinePlus alone. Sources: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt/ , https://medlineplus.gov/deepveinthrombosis.html
- Low back, emergency (cauda equina): numbness or pins and needles around the genitals, inner thighs, buttocks or back passage; new trouble peeing, leaking, or less feeling peeing or passing a stool; symptoms in both legs; pain after a serious accident. Urgent: fever or feeling unwell, severe pain that came on suddenly or is quickly worsening; see a GP: pain worse at night or not eased by rest. Sources: https://spinal.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/National-Suspected-Cauda-Equina-Pathway-February-2023-FINAL-V1-1.pdf (GIRFT pathway, 2023), https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/
- Achilles rupture: a snap or pop at the back of the ankle, not being able to rise onto the toes or push off, not being able to walk on it. Sources: https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/diseases--conditions/achilles-tendon-rupture-tear/ , https://www.ouh.nhs.uk/media/l1lhs3h3/116457achilles.pdf , https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/tendonitis/
- Neck, emergency (artery signs): sudden neck or head pain unlike any before; dizziness or unsteadiness; slurred speech; trouble swallowing; changes in vision; numbness in the face; a drooping face or eyelid. Spinal cord signs (clumsy hands, weak or heavy limbs) need a clinician, urgency unstated; a headache worsening with effort is from the neck guideline. Sources: https://hsu.repository.guildhe.ac.uk/id/eprint/188/1/International%20Framework%20for%20Examination%20of%20the%20Cervical%20Region%20for%20Potential.pdf (IFOMPT framework, authors' manuscript), https://leedscommunityhealthcare.nhs.uk/our-services-a-z/musculoskeletal-msk/neck-problems/known-and-diagnosed-neck-problems/degenerative-cervical-myelopathy/ , https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/318008/mod_resource/content/1/revisione%202017.pdf
- Rib: worsening breathlessness, fever, coughing up blood (NHS leaflet); chest-wall tenderness doesn't rule out a heart attack (12 percent in one emergency study). Sources: https://www.worcsacute.nhs.uk/leaflets/physiotherapy-advice-and-exercises-following-rib-fractures/ , https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2021/0700/p73.html
- Wrist: about a quarter of suspected scaphoid fractures with a normal first X-ray turn out to be fractures (abstract only); extreme finger stiffness, a hand oversensitive to touch or skin changes call for an urgent therapy consult after a broken wrist. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24673666/ , https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/mehta_et_al_2024_distal_radius_fracture_rehabilitation.pdf

## Foot
House judgment: the session lines, the stop signs, the off-limits `say` line (a plain day off, no claim about recovery) and both check-in signs.
- Plantar heel pain: a guideline recommends plantar fascia and calf stretching (grade A) and resistance exercise for the foot and ankle muscles (grade B), and night splints for 1 to 3 months when the first steps in the morning hurt; no loading progression or timings. This is the source of the foot's `say` line. Source: https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/Heel_Pain_Plantar_Fasciitis_revision_2023_1_.pdf (Koc and colleagues, JOSPT 2023, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38037331/)

## Ankle
Sourced: the order of the check-in signs (balance, then calf raises, then hops) follows the guideline's list of measures below, and `after_clearing` (balance work for eight weeks) follows the balance trial. House judgment: the gates themselves, since no source sets pass marks, the stop signs, and the off-limits `say` line (a plain day off: the guideline favours early loading, so no line claims rest speeds recovery).
- Most common sports injury, often recurs; an expert consensus checks pain, range, strength, confidence, balance and hopping before sport, without pass marks. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34158354/
- Brief immobilisation may ease pain; after an ankle ligament rupture, brace or tape plus exercise does best. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29514819/
- Early mobilisation helps; moderate evidence that balance training prevents another sprain, dose unstated. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28053200/
- A guideline (grade A) advises braces or taping with progressively more weight on the foot after a sprain, immobilisation of up to 10 days only for severe ones, then structured exercise at home and in clinic (active range of motion kept within comfort, stretching, neuromuscular and balance training), plus bracing and balance-focused training to prevent another sprain; it lists measures to recheck (weight-bearing lunge, eyes-closed single-leg balance, reach balance, timed single-leg hopping) but sets no pass marks. Source: https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/jospt.2021.0302.pdf (Martin and colleagues, JOSPT 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33789434/)
- A home balance programme after usual care (8 weeks, three sessions a week of up to 30 minutes on a balance board, started after returning to sport) cut repeat sprains over a year from 33 to 22 percent (522 athletes, relative risk 0.63, nine treated to prevent one); the exercises themselves aren't in the paper. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19589822/

## Calf
The calf area also holds the Achilles tendon. Sourced: the `say` line (no worse the next morning) and the reasoning behind it (rehab by response, not the calendar; loading over rest), and calf raises as the Achilles treatment. House judgment: the session lines' limits, the stop signs and the check-in signs.
- Calf strain: twenty expert sports clinicians described rehab in six phases, guided by the calf's measured capacity and how it responds to loading rather than by time; experts' views, not a trial, and no doses. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35032233/
- Achilles tendinopathy: a peer-reviewed review rates loading exercise grade A (eccentric or slow heavy work; calf raises progressing from two legs to one, then slow lowering, then off a step, then weighted), at least twice a week, and warns that complete rest may do harm; it summarises the 2018 JOSPT guideline, which couldn't be opened. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7249277/
- Heavy slow resistance and eccentric training gave equally good, lasting results over 12 weeks (58 patients; abstract only, no dose). Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26018970/

## Knee
Sourced: the `say` line and the session's hip work (hip with knee exercise for kneecap pain). House judgment: partial range and no landing, the stop signs and the check-in signs; no source gives a depth rule or a pain threshold for the knee.
- Kneecap pain: a consensus recommends hip plus knee exercise over knee alone; not for acute tears. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4975825/
- A summary of the 2019 JOSPT guideline gives combined hip and knee exercise grade A for pain and function in the short, medium and long term, hip work possibly first; no dose. The guideline itself couldn't be opened. Source: https://www.guidelinecentral.com/guideline/8381/

## Hamstring
Sourced: the `say` line (lengthening work) and `after_clearing` (a recent strain makes another several times more likely). House judgment: the eight weeks, the three-second lowering, the session lines, stop signs and check-in signs.
- Acute hamstring strain: lengthening exercises (75 elite footballers, MRI-confirmed) returned players to training in 28 days on average against 51 with conventional exercises; elite sport, supervised, no home dose. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23536466/
- A previous hamstring strain is among the strongest risk factors for another (78 studies, recent strain risk ratio 4.8), so keep hamstring strengthening in after clearing. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32299793/

## Hip
Three hip problems pull in different directions: strengthening is grade A for an arthritic hip, while hip joint pain without arthritis (impingement, a labral tear) adds activity modification, limiting deep bending, pivoting and sitting cross-legged. The delicate session takes the stricter of the two. Sourced: the `say` line (strengthening, glutes especially) and the stop signs at the front and side. House judgment: applying the impingement limits to every hip, a limp ending the session, and the check-in signs; no source gives a pain threshold or pass marks.
- Hip arthritis: an individualised exercise programme for motion, strength, function and pain (grade A), 1 to 5 times a week over 5 to 16 weeks, mostly supervised; unsupervised home exercise did worst in the trials it cites. Source: https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/Hip_pain_and_mobility_deficits_hip_osteoarthritis_2025.pdf (Koc and colleagues, JOSPT 2025)
- Hip joint pain without arthritis: activity modification plus strengthening of the hip flexors, glutes, hip rotators and trunk (grade B); one case series suggests limiting squatting, leg crossing, pivoting and floor sitting (grade C); no dose. Source: https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/jospt.2023.0302.pdf (Enseki and colleagues, JOSPT 2023)
- Pain at the outside of the hip (gluteal tendinopathy): education plus daily home abductor strengthening beat an injection and wait-and-see at 8 weeks and a year (204 people aged 35 to 70). Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5930290/ (Mellor and colleagues, BMJ 2018)

## Groin
Sourced: the `say` line (strengthening around the pelvis, for pain that has hung on). House judgment: the session lines, stop signs and check-in signs, and reading "long-standing" as two months or more in `say_when`, so a fresh strain doesn't get the line.
- Long-standing adductor groin pain: an 8-to-12-week active programme strengthening the muscles around the pelvis, especially the adductors, returned 23 athletes to sport pain-free against 4 with passive physiotherapy (68 athletes in all, odds ratio 12.7); it covers months-old pain, not a fresh strain. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9989713/

## Low back
Sourced: walking and neutral-spine work going on throughout and the `say` line (staying active), from NICE; the red flags and emergencies, from the GIRFT pathway and the NHS (Red flags, above). House judgment: the stop signs and check-in signs.
- Encourage normal activity and exercise; check other causes for new symptoms. Not re-checked on 2026-11-02 (the page refused the fetch). Source: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/Recommendations

## Rib
Little exists outside hospital care. Sourced: full, easy breathing and moving little and often, leaving out what provokes it (lifting, pushing, pulling, hard upper-body work), and the stricter pain rule; and, from the red flags, chest pain still means the heart rule even when it feels like the rib, said in the opening. House judgment: the stop signs and check-in signs.
- Rib fracture: the only systematic review covers hospital care; physiotherapy including breathing exercises was linked to less pain and fewer lung complications, at low to very low certainty; nothing on exercise after discharge. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9257723
- One NHS trust's leaflet (not a guideline): breathing exercises at least four times a day, moving little and often, no lifting, pushing or pulling that worsens the pain, no heavy lifting for at least 4 weeks and no contact sport for at least 6. Source: https://www.worcsacute.nhs.uk/leaflets/physiotherapy-advice-and-exercises-following-rib-fractures/
- Costochondritis: rest from or less of whatever provokes it, especially hard upper-body work such as rowing; most settle within weeks (grade C throughout). Source: https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2021/0700/p73.html

## Shoulder
Sourced: the `say` line (light loads work about as well as heavy). House judgment: the session lines, stop signs (an ache that builds, so pain down the arm stays the neck's sign) and check-in signs.
- Rotator cuff pain: exercise helps; light loads work about as well as heavy. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9904825/

## Elbow
Sourced (outer elbow pain, tennis elbow): forearm loading as the treatment, holds before lifts before slow lowering, stretching alone not enough, the stricter "no worse" rule, and numbness into the hand as a sign to stop (now the general stop rule's, which ends the session). House judgment: the off-limits lines and the check-in signs. The inner elbow wasn't researched.
- Lateral elbow pain: a guideline recommends resisted wrist-extensor exercise, holds, lifts or slow lowering (grade B), at a suggested 3 sets of 15 for 6 to 12 weeks, with load that doesn't make symptoms worse; nerve symptoms predict worse outcomes. Source: https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/jospt.2022.0302.pdf (Lucado and colleagues, JOSPT 2022, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36453071/)
- Benefits come early and don't clearly last: physiotherapy beat a placebo injection at 4 weeks, with no difference at 26 or 52 weeks; daily home holds beat wait-and-see on disability at 8 weeks (abstracts only). Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23385272/ , https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31425384/

## Wrist
Sourced (after a broken wrist): the clinician's timeline for wrist work, shoulder and elbow movement as part of recovery, pain on the little-finger side under load, and the red flags. Sourced in general: loading, not rest alone. House judgment: straight-wrist work only, with push-ups on handles left out while the wrist is delicate, since the catalog rates them as the floor (no source covers weight through a recovering wrist), and the check-in signs.
- Broken wrist: a guideline recommends early hand, wrist, elbow and shoulder movement (grade A), light gripping from about two weeks for stable fractures only (grade B), strengthening usually from six to eight weeks once healing allows, with caution for pain on the little-finger side; return to sport is the treating team's call. Source: https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/mehta_et_al_2024_distal_radius_fracture_rehabilitation.pdf (Mehta and colleagues, JOSPT 2024)
- Soft-tissue care runs on to loading, not just rest and ice; no timings. Source: https://blogs.bmj.com/bjsm/2019/04/26/soft-tissue-injuries-simply-need-peace-love/

## Neck
Sourced: neck movement within comfort and shoulder-blade and arm strengthening even early, staying active, and the warning signs and red flags (that pain spreading into the arm and a building headache end the session is house judgment). House judgment: no overhead pressing under a delicate limit, and both check-in signs; nothing found either way on overhead pressing. Also house judgment: the artery signs that can start mid-session (a sudden headache or neck pain unlike any before, dizziness, slurred speech, a change in vision) are spoken with the heart rule under any neck limit (`heart_rule` in limits.toml), not left to the check-in, since the listener is alone with the audio when they'd start.
- Neck pain: a guideline recommends neck range of motion plus shoulder-blade and arm strengthening even in the first six weeks (grade B), neck and shoulder-girdle endurance work from six to twelve weeks, and a return to normal activities that don't provoke it; it sets no dose. Source: https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/318008/mod_resource/content/1/revisione%202017.pdf (course copy of Blanpied and colleagues, JOSPT 2017, doi 10.2519/jospt.2017.0302)
- Chronic neck pain: moderate-quality evidence that strengthening the neck, shoulder-blade region and arms reduces pain in the short term; stretching alone may not help (abstract only). Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25629215/

## Unlisted injuries
An injury outside the catalog's areas gets no `[[limits]]` row: it's written "- Unlisted: <what>" in `me/health.md`'s prose, with an `Avoid:` line under `## Clinician rules`, and `[general] unlisted` in `knowledge/limits.toml` says how the check-in handles it. The brief names the injury and quotes `unlisted_session`: house judgment, a conservative session (moderate effort, nothing jumping, no breath holding or straining) with pain at the site stopping the move and pain that stays ending the session. After surgery or a joint replacement, and in pregnancy, nothing here was researched: the clinician's or surgeon's precautions are the only guide.

## How the filter applies limits
For maintainers; the brief's menu does all of this before a writer sees anything (`tools/catalog.py`, `tools/screen.py`).
- Each injury is a `[[limits]]` row in `me/health.md`. Movements rate each area's load from 0 to 3 (`loads`, and `support` for the resting side). **Off limits** rules out loads of 1 or more; **delicate** rules out 2 or more. Both rule out all `impact` work and any `fast` work that loads the area at all (fast seated punching stays in for a delicate foot; jumping never does), so off limits is never looser than delicate.
- The foot, ankle and calf count as one chain: fast work that loads any of them is out under a limit on one.
- A row's `flags` rule out more; its `rehab` ids bring in the catalog's rehab drills (the entries marked `rehab`: ankle circles and alphabet, the band ankle drills, towel scrunch, single-leg balance), which is how seated ankle range of motion and band eversion come in under an ankle limit. Naming any other movement there changes nothing: a calf raise under a calf or ankle limit stays with the physio's plan. A left or right limit keeps healthy-side work.
- Off limits on the foot or ankle also leaves out anything done standing, kneeling or on all fours, where the top of the foot rests pointed; pedaling and a bench press's leg drive count as light ankle loads.
- Off limits on the low back also leaves out the `spine-flexion` and `spine-twist` flags.
- The catalog rates every overhead press neck 2, so a delicate neck rules overhead pressing out.
- A row with `cleared = <date>` is no limit at all: the filter skips it, and the brief quotes the area's `after_clearing` until that many weeks after the date.
