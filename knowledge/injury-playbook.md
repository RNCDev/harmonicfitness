# Injury Playbook

How to program around an injury or limitation. This is a general guide for a healthy adult working around a known, already-assessed problem, not a substitute for a clinician. Anything a doctor or physical therapist has told the user overrides this file; record those instructions in the profile's injury entry and follow them literally.

## Procedure for every active injury
1. Identify the body part, side, what happened, and roughly when.
2. Determine the phase from the user's report today:
   - Protect (first days, or pain at rest, visible swelling, limping): avoid loading the area entirely. Train everything else.
   - Restore (pain only with certain movements, swelling settling): gentle, pain-free range of motion for the injured area; normal training elsewhere with the contraindications below.
   - Rebuild (walking normally, mild or no pain): progressive, controlled loading of the area, still avoiding high-speed or impact work.
   - Return (cleared or symptom-free in daily life): reintroduce impact and speed gradually, one variable at a time. If unsure, choose the more conservative phase and say so in chat.
3. List banned movements (below) for that body part and phase, and check every chosen exercise against the list, including warm-up and finisher. Hidden culprits are common: burpees, mountain climbers, jumping jacks, lunges, and standing kettlebell swings all load the ankle and knee.
4. Choose substitutions that train the same quality without the banned stress.
5. Script safety cues into the audio: one mention in the opening segment, and a short reminder in any segment where the injured area is near the action ("keep that left foot planted and quiet").
6. Include a stop rule in the opening: pain should stay mild, around two or three out of ten at most, and should not be worse the next morning. Sharp pain, new swelling, numbness, or giving way means stop that movement.
7. Red flags to mention in chat (not in the audio) if the user reports them: inability to bear weight, rapidly increasing swelling, numbness or tingling, a joint that gives way, or pain that keeps getting worse after a week or two. These deserve a clinician visit before more training.

## Ankle (sprain or strain; specify side)
Protect phase, banned: all jumping, hopping, skipping, running, jogging in place, burpees, mountain climbers, jumping jacks, lunges of any kind, step-ups, calf raises on the injured side, standing single-leg work on the injured side, lateral shuffles, standing on unstable surfaces, pushing off the foot (including standing on the bike pedals or out-of-saddle efforts), and any rolling of the foot inward. Allowed: upper body seated or on a bench, floor core (dead bugs, glute bridges with both feet down only if pain-free, side planks from the knee), seated or lying leg work that doesn't load the ankle (straight-leg raises, side-lying hip abduction, clamshells, hamstring curls on the floor with the injured foot unloaded), single-leg work on the healthy side if balance is held with a hand on support, upper-body cardio (seated battle-rope style arm drills, seated shadow boxing, arm cranking).

Restore phase adds: seated ankle alphabet and circles, gentle seated calf work with a band, seated stationary bike with light resistance and the heel or midfoot centered on the pedal, both-leg glute bridges, supported squats to a box if pain-free.

Rebuild phase adds: double-leg calf raises, supported single-leg balance on the injured side (hand on a wall), goblet squats, split squats with the injured leg behind only if comfortable, moderate bike resistance seated.

Return phase adds: light hops on two feet, then single-leg hops, then lateral movement, one at a time across sessions.

## Knee
Banned early: deep loaded knee bending, lunges, jumping, pivoting, kneeling on the sore knee, high bike resistance. Substitutes: hip hinges (Romanian deadlifts if pain-free), glute bridges, hamstring curls, straight-leg raises, side-lying hip work, upper body, core, light seated bike with seat set slightly high.

## Lower back
Banned early: loaded spinal flexion (sit-ups, crunches), heavy hinges, twisting under load, overhead pressing standing. Substitutes: dead bugs, bird dogs, side planks, glute bridges, supported rows (chest on bench), walking or easy bike, seated presses with back support. Favor neutral spine and bracing cues.

## Shoulder
Banned early: overhead pressing, dips, wide-grip pressing, pull-ups, kipping, lying on the sore side, behind-the-neck anything. Substitutes: neutral-grip floor press with light weight if pain-free, rows to the lower ribs, band external rotation, scapular work, lower-body and core training (avoid planks if they hurt; use dead bugs).

## Wrist and hand
Banned early: push-ups and planks on hands, front squats with a front-rack grip, heavy gripping. Substitutes: forearm planks, push-ups on fists or dumbbell handles only if pain-free, goblet hold with forearms, lower-body bodyweight work, bike.

## Neck
Banned early: shrugs, overhead loading, headstands, crunches with neck pulling, fast head turning. Substitutes: most lower-body and core work with neutral head position; cue "chin gently tucked, eyes on the floor a few feet ahead".

## Hip
Banned early: deep hip flexion under load, wide-stance squats, lateral lunges, kicking. Substitutes: partial-range bridges, upper body, seated bike with a high seat, core in neutral.

## Unlisted injuries
Ask what movement hurts and what doesn't. Ban anything that loads, stretches, or impacts the injured tissue; train around it; keep it conservative; and recommend checking with a clinician if it's new, severe, or not improving.
