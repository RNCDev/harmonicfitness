# Equipment Library

Movement menus by equipment, each with one or two spoken form cues. Choose from the kits the user has today, after removing anything the injury playbook bans. Tags in parentheses describe what each movement loads, to make injury filtering fast: (impact), (ankle), (knee), (back), (shoulder), (wrist), (standing).

## No equipment (bodyweight, floor space)
Cardio and warm-up: marching in place (standing, ankle), arm circles, jumping jacks (impact, ankle, standing), high knees (impact, ankle), shadow boxing standing (standing, ankle) or seated, inchworms (wrist, shoulder), seated arm pumping drills. Lower body: air squats (knee, ankle, standing), reverse lunges (knee, ankle), glute bridges, single-leg glute bridges, wall sit (knee), side-lying leg raises, clamshells, straight-leg raises, donkey kicks from all fours (wrist). Upper body: push-ups or incline push-ups (wrist, shoulder), pike push-ups (shoulder, wrist), tricep dips off a chair (shoulder, wrist), prone Y T W raises. Core: dead bugs, bird dogs (wrist), forearm plank, side plank from knee or foot, hollow hold (back), reverse crunches (back), bicycle crunches (back). Cues: "Ribs down, low back gently pressed into the floor." "Push the floor away." "Squeeze the glutes at the top and hold one beat."

## Dumbbells
Lower body: goblet squat (knee, ankle, standing), Romanian deadlift (back, standing), split squat (knee, ankle), step-up (knee, ankle), dumbbell glute bridge, seated calf raise (ankle). Upper body: floor press, bench press, single-arm row with hand on bench, bent-over row (back, standing), seated shoulder press (shoulder), lateral raise, hammer curl, overhead tricep extension (shoulder), renegade row (wrist, shoulder). Full body: thrusters (knee, shoulder, standing), man makers (impact, wrist), farmer carry (standing, ankle), suitcase carry (standing, ankle). Seated-only options, useful for lower-limb injuries: seated shoulder press, seated curls, seated lateral raise, chest-supported row on an incline bench, floor press, dumbbell pullover lying on the bench. Cues: "Hinge from the hips like you're closing a car door with your backside." "Elbow drives toward the back pocket." "Weight stays over the middle of the foot."

## Kettlebell
Swing (back, ankle, standing), goblet squat (knee, ankle, standing), halo (shoulder), around-the-world pass, single-arm row, floor press, Turkish get-up (shoulder, wrist, ankle), kettlebell deadlift (back, standing), seated or half-kneeling press (knee, shoulder), windmill (back, shoulder), suitcase carry (standing, ankle).

## Stationary bike
Intervals by cadence and resistance. Write cadence in words ("a cadence around ninety"). Effort language: easy, moderate, hard, all-out, as a number out of ten if helpful. Formats: steady endurance, sixty-second surges with sixty easy, tabata-style twenty on ten off (harder to script; announce each change), resistance ladders, cadence pyramids. Seated is ankle-friendly when resistance is low to moderate and the foot is centered; standing climbs and sprints load the ankle (ankle). Clip-in or cage removal: if the injured foot can't safely clip in or twist out, use the bike with sneakers on platform pedals or skip it. Cues: "Quiet upper body, relax the grip." "Smooth circles, not stomping." "Shoulders down away from the ears."

## Hockey stick and dryland ball
Stickhandling works hand speed, coordination, and wrist and forearm endurance, and sitting down to do it makes good ankle-safe conditioning. Seated, on the bench or a chair: side-to-side dribbles in front of you, front-to-back dribbles beside one foot, figure eights around a shoe or water bottle, wide and narrow dribbles, and toe drags. Keep the bottom hand loose and quiet. Standing (standing, ankle): the same drills in an athletic stance, adding weight shifts, puck-protection turns (ankle), and walking stickhandling (ankle). Shooting motion without release, as a core rotation drill: a wrist-shot follow-through, low and controlled (back). Never wind up overhead or take slap-shot swings (shoulder). Cues: "Soft hands, the ball whispers on the blade." "Eyes up, feel it, don't watch it." "Elbows away from the ribs so the hands can move."

## Rowing machine
Full-body cardio (back, ankle via the foot stretcher push, knee). Strokes per minute in words. Cue "legs, then body, then arms; arms, body, then legs on the way back".

## Resistance bands
Pull-aparts, face pulls, band rows, band chest press, external rotation, banded clamshells, monster walks (ankle, standing), banded ankle work for rehab (alphabet, eversion, inversion only if allowed in the current phase), Pallof press for anti-rotation core.

## Bench
Step-ups (knee, ankle), box squats (knee, ankle, standing), Bulgarian split squat (knee, ankle), incline push-ups (wrist, shoulder), bench dips (shoulder, wrist), chest-supported rows, seated work, hip thrusts with shoulders on the bench.

## Mat and mobility
World's greatest stretch (wrist, knee), cat-cow, thread the needle, ninety-ninety hip switches (knee), child's pose, supine twist, figure-four stretch, hamstring stretch with a strap, thoracic extension over a foam roller, box breathing.

## Hotel room (no gym)
Typically bodyweight, a chair, a bed edge, a towel, maybe a band. Chair: incline push-ups (wrist, shoulder), step-ups (knee, ankle), dips (shoulder, wrist), Bulgarian split squats (knee, ankle). Towel: towel rows around a door handle only if the door is solid and closed toward the user, towel slides on hard floor for hamstring curls. Keep noise low; no jumping in hotel rooms.

## Hotel gym (assume basic)
Hotel gyms vary, so plan for the common minimum and offer a swap for anything less certain. Usually present: a dumbbell rack (often only up to about fifty pounds), an adjustable bench, a treadmill, an elliptical, an upright or recumbent bike, a mat area, and sometimes a cable machine or a Smith machine. Cardio: upright bike or recumbent bike (seated, ankle-friendly at low to moderate resistance; the recumbent is the gentler option for an injured ankle), elliptical (standing, ankle), treadmill walking or incline walking (standing, ankle), treadmill running (impact, ankle). Strength: everything in the Dumbbells and Bench sections, plus cable work if a machine is there (see Full gym). Scripting notes: say "if your hotel has a cable machine, use it; if not, grab a dumbbell and do this instead" for anything beyond dumbbells and a bench. Heavy dumbbells may not exist, so program effort by reps and tempo ("pick a weight that leaves two reps in the tank") rather than specific pounds.

## Full gym
Assume a complete commercial gym: squat racks and barbells, plate-loaded and selectorized machines, cable stations, a full dumbbell rack, benches, rowers, bikes, treadmills, sometimes a ski erg, assault bike, sled, and turf. Barbell: back squat (knee, ankle, back, standing), front squat (knee, ankle, wrist), deadlift and Romanian deadlift (back, standing), bench press, incline bench press (shoulder), barbell row (back, standing), hip thrust with the barbell across the hips. Machines, the most injury-friendly options: leg press (knee, ankle through the push; light and with the foot flat only once the ankle allows), leg extension (knee), seated or lying hamstring curl, hip abduction and adduction, chest press, seated row, lat pulldown, assisted pull-up (shoulder), pec deck, rear-delt fly, back extension (back), seated calf raise (ankle). Cables: face pulls, cable rows, single-arm cable press, Pallof press, woodchops (back), straight-arm pulldown, tricep pushdown, cable curls, cable pull-through (back). Cables can be set up seated or half-kneeling (knee) to keep weight off a sore lower leg. Cardio: rowing machine (back, ankle, knee), ski erg (standing, back), assault or air bike (seated, ankle when pushing hard), upright and recumbent bikes, treadmill, stair climber (ankle, knee, standing), sled push or pull (ankle, knee, standing). Scripting notes: a club gym is shared, so a station may be taken. For every main movement, name a fallback in the setup cue ("leg curl machine, or if it's busy, a dumbbell held between your feet on the bench"). Moving between areas of a big gym takes time; group movements by zone (cable zone, then machine row, then the bike) and give transitions at least twenty to thirty seconds. Barbell setup and plate loading takes a minute or more, so give barbell work longer segments with the setup inside the segment, or skip barbells in short sessions. Never script anything that requires a spotter.
