# The movement library build-out (0.41.0)

Covers the evidence behind the catalog entries finished or added in 0.41.0 and 0.41.1: the 49 drafts finished (loads, levels, coaching) and 63 new movements for the equipment added in this release (pull-up bar, dip station, suspension trainer, medicine and slam balls, box, sliders, stability ball, ab wheel, battle ropes, landmine, trap bar, jump rope) and for gaps in the home kit (standing calf raises, the lunges that were refused before, the Copenhagen plank and others). It doesn't cover session dosing (see `2026-10-21-session-structure.md`) or the movements finished before 0.41.0.

Researched in six batches on 2026-10-30, each citing only pages opened. ExRx, Physiopedia and several journal sites refused automated reads (HTTP 403 or a browser check), so they aren't cited; papers were read through PubMed, PMC or Europe PMC records, marked "abstract only" where so. Few sources give joint loads on the catalog's 0-to-3 scale: most ratings follow the catalog's own rules and were rated up where unsure, as each batch's "Not confirmed" list says.

Merge decisions (made after the batches, not from a source):
- `towel-hamstring-curl` keeps its id (old logs name it) and now takes sliders or a towel; batch E's duplicate `slider-hamstring-curl` was dropped.
- `burpee` gave its "squat thrust" aliases to the new `squat-thrust`, its easier version.
- The `hanging` flag stays on movements done hanging from a bar (pull-ups, hangs, knee raises); rows with the feet on the floor and dips held on straight arms don't take it, and their shoulder ratings filter them.
- Refused (`[unlisted]`) for a listener coached by ear with no one watching: Olympic lifts and snatches, kipping and butterfly pull-ups, muscle-ups, sissy squats, dragon flags, Jefferson curls, behind-the-neck presses, upright rows, double unders, and full neck circles (the AMA advises half circles; batch A).
- The jump rope's alternate-foot step was not added as a gentler version: Lin et al. 2022 (batch C) found it lands harder than the two-foot bounce.

### Findings that set loads, levels or coaching
- straight-leg-raise: tighten the thigh first, knee straight, lift to the other knee's height (UMass Memorial); a knee-rehab staple, so knee = 1, hip = 2 for hip flexion under the leg's weight, low-back = 1.
- pillow-squeeze-bridge: groin rehab uses the squeeze with pain kept low (Pure Physiotherapy), so groin = 2 (a delicate groin excludes it); bridge loads as glute-bridge.
- face-down-hip-extension: larger lower-back moments than back extensions (Cissik summary of Lawrence 2019), low-back = 2, level experienced; head down, no swing (Endurance Magazine).
- towel-hamstring-curl: the slider bridge is among the highest-hamstring-activity eccentric exercises (JSCR 29(3), search result), hamstring = 3, level experienced (glute bridge is its novice version), four seconds a rep for the slow slide.
- bicycle-crunch: slow, lift from the belly, don't pull the head (ACE; Hinge Health); head held up the whole set, so neck = 2; flexion with twist, low-back = 2, flags spine-flexion and spine-twist; primary pattern changed to rotation.
- woodchop: ACE rates the standing chop advanced and notes straight arms load the spine; hips and back foot pivot (ACE, search results), low-back = 2, shoulder = 2, hip = 2.
- monster-walk: band at knees or ankles, quarter squat (ACE); more distal placement raises glute medius activity (Cambridge 2012); knee = 2 for the held quarter squat.
- side-lying-leg-raise: lift only until the hips tip (ACE), hip = 3, novice.
- back-extension: pad below the hip bones, rise to a straight line and no further (search results); back extensions take the lumbar spine through more range (Cissik), low-back = 3.
- cable-pull-through: glutes primary, low back secondary (StrengthLog); taught to beginners as a hinge (search results), novice; grip flag for the rope.
- front-squat: high elbows, rack at shoulder height, open hand if wrists complain (Squat University); less knee compression than the back squat but same muscles (Gullett 2009); barbell lift, advanced; safety bars set so no spotter is ever needed.
- ninety-ninety-hip-switch: knees at right angles, heels down (Prehab Guys); knee twisting is the common complaint (search results), so knee = 2.
- strap-hamstring-stretch: lift only as far as comfortable, breathe (UMass Memorial).
- foam-roller-thoracic-extension: motion from the upper back, not the low back, chin tucked (Physitrack, Reinold); the roller presses the rib cage, rib = 2.
- sphinx-stretch: prone extension, stop on low-back pain (ACE cobra); weight on the forearms, elbow = 2 and shoulder = 2 by the catalog's forearm rule.
- standing-quad-stretch: wall for balance, knees together (ACE); a one-leg stance, single-leg flag and support on the standing leg.
- neck-rolls: the AMA recommends half circles and warns against dropping the head back, so the entry is now half circles in front only, renamed "half neck circles", neck = 2.
- inchworm: ACE rates its version advanced (with a push-up); without the push-up it's set to experienced; wrists and shoulders bear weight in the plank (2), hinge (low-back = 2).

### Not confirmed
- ExRx.net and Physiopedia refused the fetch (HTTP 403), so no ExRx classifications back these entries.
- Joint-load numbers (the 0 to 3 ratings) come from the catalog's rules and the movement mechanics, not from measured joint loads, except front-squat (Gullett) and the back extension and reverse hyper comparison (Cissik's summary). All rated up where unsure: neck = 2 on bicycle-crunch and neck-rolls, groin = 2 on pillow-squeeze-bridge, knee = 2 on ninety-ninety-hip-switch, rib = 2 on the roller, shoulder = 2 on sphinx-stretch, spine-flexion on inchworm.
- The sliding hamstring curl study (JSCR 29(3) 772-778) came from a search result page only; the full abstract page would not load.
- Levels without a source rating: face-down-hip-extension (experienced, chosen for the lumbar moment), towel-hamstring-curl (experienced), back-extension (experienced), cable-pull-through (novice), ninety-ninety-hip-switch, strap-hamstring-stretch, foam-roller-thoracic-extension, standing-quad-stretch (novice). woodchop is set to experienced although ACE rates its medicine-ball chop advanced; inchworm is set to experienced although ACE rates its push-up version advanced.
- The draft's monster-walk is coached as side steps (a lateral band walk), as its setup had it; ACE describes a monster walk as diagonal steps forward and back.

### Findings that set loads, levels or coaching
- bench-dip: shoulder 3 because the bench dip asks the most shoulder extension of the dips and the front capsule and ligament take the stress at the bottom (Physical Therapy First, ACE Q and A, search page). Shallow range cued in the setup, cues, rep and first fault. Elbow 3 (the moving joint, triceps-dominant, NASM); wrist 2 (body weight on extended wrists). - turkish-get-up: advanced (ACE); steps follow ACE; seconds_per_rep 20 for the full up and down.
- kettlebell-windmill: advanced (ACE High Windmill); low-back 3 and hip 3 for the loaded side bend and turn under an overhead weight; spine-twist and spine-flexion flags.

- around-the-world-pass: novice, because Barbend teaches it as the step before the halo and Human Kinetics calls it a warm-up (search page); hips still (Physitrack). Primary pattern changed to anti-rotation, since the trunk resists the pass rather than turning.

- thruster: squat plus press (Prehab Guys); vertical-push added as a second pattern; experienced (a loaded compound lift).
- man-maker: advanced (it holds a renegade row, a clean and a thruster, search page and Life Time); the coaching steps rather than jumps, so no impact flag.
- single-arm-cable-press: experienced (ACE intermediate).
- band-chest-press: novice (search page); staggered stance and straight wrists (ECU Health).
- incline-bench-press: advanced (a barbell lift, per the catalog rule); about thirty degrees (PMC7579505); the no-spotter line matches the bench press entry.
- barbell-row: low-back 3 from the largest lumbar spine load of the rows tested (PubMed 19197209); advanced.

- box-jump: Advanced (NASM). Needs `box`. Step down every rep (NASM). - suspension-hamstring-curl: gastrocnemius is the most active muscle (PMC8385437), so calves listed; hips kept up for the whole set (Physitrack); experienced, matching lying-dumbbell-hamstring-curl.

- trap-bar-deadlift: experienced, not advanced. - ab-wheel-rollout: shoulder 3 and overhead flag because the arms open toward 150 degrees under a body-weight lever (Marchetti); low-back 2 for Sussex's strain warning; knee 2 for kneeling; experienced.

### Findings that set loads, levels or coaching
- prone-lat-pulldown: arms work overhead off the floor, so shoulder 2 and the overhead flag (rule 2); prone arm lift extends the upper back, so low-back 1 and neck 1, matching the catalog's prone-y-t-w-raise. - wall-angel: patterns = ["mobility"] only. Overhead to shoulder 2; holding the low back to the wall low-back 1; head on the wall neck 1; thoracic extension rib 1. Back-to-wall version sourced (Physitrack, Healthline).
- wall-push-up-plus: made a separate novice entry, since Ludewig 2004 compared wall, knee and standard push-up plus and suggests progressing through the modified versions, and Castelein 2016 found the wall version clearly lower in serratus than the floor. Straight arms, so elbow 1; hands on the wall wrist 2, shoulder 2 as on wall-push-up.
- push-up-plus: about three quarters of body weight through the hands (PMC4327800), the movement is about the shoulder blade, so shoulder 3; feet and wrists rated as on push-up. Experienced, since it has a novice version (the header's push-up rule). Setup says "hands and toes" rather than naming a push-up position, which would trip the scanner.
- side-lying-open-book: knees stacked high at hip height limit lumbar rotation (Prehab Guys, NISMAT), so rib 2 (the rotation) but low-back 1. Shoulder 1 for the opening stretch, as thread-the-needle; support hip 1 and shoulder 1 for the side lain on.
- overhead-carry: shoulder 3 (load overhead with lockout), elbow 2 (locked under load), wrist 2 (Breaking Muscle's wrist warning), low-back 2 (arching and leaning under an overhead load, Cressey). Level experienced, matching overhead-press, since the weight is held over the head and a failed lockout is a real risk; ACE rates its bottoms-up waiter's carry Advanced.
- front-rack-carry: made one-sided (one weight, swap halfway) as the Prehab Guys' unilateral version, so it works with a single kettlebell or dumbbell; low-back 2 for a load held in front and offset (rule 2), support low-back 1 as on suitcase-carry. Novice per the catalog's carries rule.
- ninety-ninety-breathing: needs = [] because the wall version (Functional Movement) needs nothing; setup offers a chair or the bed edge as the alternative (Physitrack). Loads {} as the other breathing entries; floor flag since it needs lying on the floor. Hold sixty, as the other breathing entries.

- Rio E, et al. Isometric exercise induces analgesia and reduces inhibition in patellar tendinopathy. Br J Sports Med 2015;49(19):1277-83, PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25979840/ — abstract only. ### Findings that set loads, levels or coaching
- spanish-squat: knee 3. The exercise exists to load the patellar tendon (Basas case series; Welsh Athletics), so knee 3 under the catalog's rule (the joint the movement is about, bodyweight counts), matching wall-sit. Upright shins and trunk keep ankle at 1 and the low back unrated; hip 1 as in wall-sit at a similar depth. - spanish-squat, is knee 3 right? Yes for the catalog's purpose: a delicate knee excluding it is the safe default, since a generic sore knee should not get a deliberate patellar tendon load. The listeners it is meant for (patellar tendinopathy) are better served by a separate route than a lower rating: a `rehab = true` copy (or this entry flagged rehab) that a limit's `rehab` list names, which lets it through for that listener only. Rio 2015 shows isometrics are analgesic in tendinopathy, which argues for allowing it by clinician choice, not for underrating it.
- split-squat-hold: loads and support copied from split-squat (same position, held at the bottom). Novice as split-squat is; hold 30 per the header. Hover the back knee rather than rest it, so no kneeling flag. Isometric format from Rio 2015 and the 2020 trial (abstracts); no source on this exact exercise was found.
- knee-to-wall-ankle-mobilization: ankle 3 (the joint the movement is about, at end range under body weight; the seated band dorsiflexion entry is already ankle 3), knee 2 (bent and bearing weight), calf 2 (the soleus is stretched under load; standing-calf-stretch rates calf 2). - knee-to-wall-ankle-mobilization: `rehab` left false (unlike the Rehab: ankle entries). It is a common general warm-up drill, and rehab = true would hide it from every listener whose limit doesn't name it. - nordic-hamstring-curl: hamstring 3, knee 2 (kneeling on the pad), ankle 1 (pinned), low-back 1 and hip 1 (rigid trunk), wrist 2, elbow 2, shoulder 2 for the catch at the bottom (Physitrack: fall forward and push back up). Experienced as asked: the failure position is sitting back toward the heels, not a fall. Included because a peer-reviewed training study (Alonso-Fernandez 2019) and a university-registered trial protocol describe it.
